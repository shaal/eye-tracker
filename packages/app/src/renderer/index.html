Debug & diagnostics

Work down this list in order. Each view rules out one cause, and a cause ruled out here saves recalibrating for something calibration cannot fix.

1 · Eye zoom — is the signal there at all?

Your entire gaze range spans about 58 px of iris travel, so one pixel of landmark wobble is roughly 33 px of cursor error. The green outline should hug your iris and sit still when you hold a fixation. If it shimmers, nothing downstream can be accurate.

—

2 · Signal scope — noise, lag, or a stuck clamp?

Red is what the model produced, blue is what the filter let through. Fuzzy red = sensor noise. Blue trailing red = smoothing lag. Flat blue while red moves = the fixation clamp is holding. Green bands mark clamp holds, red ticks mark low-quality frames.

—

3 · Validation — how wrong is it, and where?

Tests the model at 13 points it was not calibrated on. Arrows show systematic error (fixable by recalibrating); ellipses show random scatter (not fixable by recalibrating). Takes about 25 seconds.

4 · Live probe — what changed since it last worked?

Parks a dot on screen and reports the offset live. Look at it, then change one thing at a time — lean back, turn your head, dim the light. Whatever makes the number jump is your culprit.

5 · Calibration scatter — was the fit ever possible?

Every calibration sample plotted in gaze-feature space, coloured by target, with the fixation grid drawn as a mesh. A recognisable (if skewed or mirrored) grid means the signal separated the targets. Merged clusters mean it never did, and no amount of refitting will help. Crossing mesh lines mean the mapping folds. Hollow points were rejected as outliers; grey points are head-motion targets, which overlap the grid by design and are excluded from the numbers.

Both axes share one scale, so a short vertical band means the vertical signal really is smaller — the eyelids crop the iris exactly as you look up and down, which makes gy the harder axis.

Run a calibration to populate this.

6 · Pose drift — how far from where you calibrated?

Per-axis distance from your calibration pose, in standard deviations. Past ~3σ on any axis the model is extrapolating and accuracy degrades. This is usually the answer to "it worked a minute ago".
